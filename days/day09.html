9Thermistor Thermometer

A thermistor is a resistor that changes with temperature. With yesterday's voltage-divider trick and one famous equation, you'll turn an ADC reading into degrees Celsius — and show "cold / cosy / warm" on the traffic light.

🧰You need

  • Pico W + breadboard
  • KY-013 Analog temperature sensor
  • LED Traffic Light module (from Day 1)
  • 8 jumper wires

🔍The NTC thermistor

The KY-013 contains an NTC ("negative temperature coefficient") thermistor: when it gets warmer, its resistance goes down. At 25 °C it is 10 kΩ. Together with a fixed 10 kΩ resistor on the board it forms a voltage divider, just like the LDR yesterday.

🔌Wiring

Known quirk: on many KY-013 boards the + and − labels are swapped. That doesn't damage anything here — it just flips the divider. Step 2 below detects which way round yours is.

1. Read the ratio

We don't actually need volts. Because the divider is powered from the same 3.3 V that the ADC uses as its reference, the reading as a fraction of full scale is all we need (and the exact supply voltage cancels out).

4. Resistance → temperature: the Beta equation

Thermistors follow a curve described by the Beta equation: 1/T = 1/T0 + ln(R/R0) / B, with temperatures in Kelvin. For this sensor R0 = 10 kΩ at T0 = 25 °C (298.15 K), and B ≈ 3950.

It won't be lab-accurate — the parts have a few percent tolerance — but within a degree or two is typical. If it's off by a constant amount compared to a room thermometer, add an OFFSET.

5. Cosy-meter on the traffic light

Green below 26 °C, yellow from 26, red from 30. Warm the sensor with your fingers (or breathe on it) to climb up the scale.

Note the compact show(t >= 30, 26 <= t < 30, t < 26): comparisons give True/False, which Pin.value() accepts like 1 and 0.

⭐Exercise: "Someone is touching me!" alarm

A thermometer that notices change:

  1. Track the minimum and maximum temperature since start-up and print them every 5 seconds.
  2. Calculate the rate of change in °C per second over the last 5 seconds. If it rises faster than 0.15 °C/s, flash the red LED quickly ("touch detected!"). If it falls faster than 0.15 °C/s, flash yellow ("cooling down").
  3. Otherwise show steady green.
Hint

Sample every 0.25 s and keep the last 20 readings in a list (history.append(t), then history.pop(0) if it grows too long). Rate = (history[-1] - history[0]) / 5.

Show solution

The list history acts as a 5-second ring buffer. Comparing newest and oldest readings gives a slope that's much less noisy than comparing two neighbouring readings.
