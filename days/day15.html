15Cookie Jar Guard

Invisible infrared light protects the Christmas cookies: an obstacle sensor notices approaching hands and a light barrier counts every cookie that leaves. You'll practise counting with interrupts and measuring how long things take.

🧰You need

  • Pico W + breadboard
  • KY-010 Light barrier (fork sensor)
  • KY-032 Obstacle avoidance sensor
  • KY-012 Active buzzer
  • A strip of paper or card
  • 10 jumper wires

🔍Two kinds of infrared sensing

KY-010 light barrier

An IR LED shines across the slot onto a phototransistor. Anything that blocks the slot — a piece of card, a tooth on a wheel — interrupts the beam. Printers use these to detect paper.

KY-032 obstacle sensor

An IR LED and receiver side by side, looking forward. If something within a few centimetres reflects the light, the output goes LOW. A potentiometer sets the range. The jumper on the board should be in place (it keeps the sensor enabled), so we don't need the EN pin.

Your phone camera can see infrared! Point it at the KY-032's clear LED — you'll see it glow purple-ish on screen.

🔌Wiring

Some KY-032 boards order their pins GND + OUT EN — check the labels.

📓Code, step by step

1. Test both sensors

Slide paper through the slot, wave your hand in front of the obstacle sensor. If the obstacle sensor always reads 0 or never changes, turn its potentiometer slowly until the small "obstacle" LED goes off with nothing in front, and on when your hand is about 5 cm away.

2. Counting with an interrupt

The KY-010 output changes when the beam is blocked. As usual we remember the idle state (empty slot) and count each time the beam gets blocked. An interrupt makes sure fast objects aren't missed.

3. How long was the beam blocked?

Measuring the blocking time tells you about the object: a slow hand blocks it longer than a quick flick. We record the moment the beam is blocked and calculate the duration when it's clear again.

Appending to a list inside an interrupt handler works on the Pico because MicroPython's pin interrupts are "soft" by default (the handler runs a moment later, between normal instructions). Keep handlers small anyway.

4. The guard

A hand near the jar triggers a warning beep. Every cookie that passes the barrier is counted and reported with how long it took.

🧠What you learned

  • Transmissive (fork) vs reflective IR sensing.
  • Counting events and measuring pulse durations with interrupts.
  • Using None as "not started yet".

⭐Exercise: Catch the cookie! (reaction game)

  1. Wait a random time between 2 and 6 seconds, then switch on the on-board LED and give a short beep — "Go!".
  2. The player waves a hand in front of the obstacle sensor as fast as possible. Measure the reaction time in milliseconds.
  3. If they wave before the signal, it's a false start: long beep, round doesn't count.
  4. Play 5 rounds, then print each time, the best and the average.
Hint

random.randint(2000, 6000) gives a random number of milliseconds. During the waiting period, keep checking the sensor so you can detect a false start. Measure from the "Go!" moment with ticks_ms().

Show solution

Typical human reaction times are 200–300 ms. The 40 ms beep happens before timing starts, which slightly favours the player — move go = time.ticks_ms() above the beep for a fairer game.
