23Heartbeat

Shine infrared light through your fingertip and you can see your heart beating — as a tiny wiggle in a big signal. Today is a lesson in signal processing: dig out that wiggle, find the beats, and beep like a hospital monitor.

🧰You need

  • Pico W + breadboard
  • KY-039 Heartbeat sensor
  • KY-006 Passive buzzer
  • 5 jumper wires

🔍How it works

The KY-039 has an IR LED on one side and a phototransistor on the other. Put your fingertip between them. Each heartbeat pushes a little more blood into your finger, which absorbs a little more light. The change is tiny — often less than 1 % of the signal — so most of the work is in software. This technique is called photoplethysmography (PPG); smartwatches use it too.

Expect to experiment. This sensor is sensitive to room light, finger pressure and movement. Do it in a dim room, rest your finger lightly (don't squeeze), and keep still. Cupping your other hand over it helps a lot. It's a fun experiment, not a medical device.

🔌Wiring

📓Code, step by step

1. The raw signal

Open the plotter, place your finger and wait. You'll see a big, slowly drifting value. Look closely for small regular bumps — about one per second.

2. Steady sampling

For measuring rates, samples must be evenly spaced. sleep_ms(20) plus the time our code takes would drift, so we schedule against absolute deadlines (the trick from Day 22). Each sample also averages 8 readings to reduce noise.

3. Remove the drift

The heartbeat is a fast wiggle riding on a slow drift. A slow moving average (the baseline) follows the drift but not the beats; subtracting it leaves just the wiggle. This is a simple high-pass filter. A short average on top smooths out the high-frequency noise (a low-pass filter).

The plot should now wiggle around zero with a clear rhythm. On some boards the beats point down instead of up — the next step handles both.

4. Detect beats

A beat is where the signal crosses a threshold going in the beat's direction. The threshold adapts: it's 50 % of the recent peak size, which slowly decays so it can follow weaker signals. A refractory period of 300 ms ignores double-triggers (no heart beats faster than 200 bpm at rest).

5. BPM and beeps

Beats per minute = 60 000 / interval in ms. A single interval is jumpy, so we average the last 5 plausible ones (between 40 and 180 bpm).

Check yourself: count your pulse at your wrist for 15 seconds and multiply by 4. How close is the Pico?

🧠What you learned

  • Photoplethysmography; extracting a small signal from a large one.
  • Fixed-rate sampling; high-pass and low-pass filtering with moving averages.
  • Adaptive thresholds, refractory periods, rejecting implausible values.

⭐Exercise: a smarter pulse monitor

  1. Finger detection: the raw value changes a lot when a finger is placed or removed. If the baseline jumps by more than 10 % within half a second, or no beat for 3 seconds, print "place finger…" and reset the interval history.
  2. Robust BPM: use the median of the last 7 intervals instead of the mean. One missed or extra beat then barely changes the result.
  3. Statistics: after 30 seconds with a finger, print min, max and average BPM for the session.
