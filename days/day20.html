20Joystick Colour Wheel

A thumb joystick is two potentiometers and a button. Today you'll read both axes, turn the stick's angle into a colour on the RGB LED with a bit of trigonometry — and build a game of Simon Says in the exercise.

🧰You need

  • Pico W + breadboard
  • KY-023 Joystick module
  • KY-016 RGB LED
  • 10 jumper wires

🔍Inside the joystick

Moving the stick left/right turns one potentiometer (VRx), up/down turns another (VRy). Each pot is a voltage divider, so the output goes from 0 V at one end to the supply voltage at the other, with about half in the middle. Pressing down on the stick clicks a button (SW) that connects to ground.

The pin is labelled +5V, but connect it to the 3.3 V rail! With 5 V the outputs would exceed what the Pico's analog pins can take.

🔌Wiring

📓Code, step by step

1. Read the stick

The button has no resistor on the module, so we switch on the Pico's internal pull-up with Pin.PULL_UP: the pin reads 1 normally and 0 when pressed.

2. Centre and scale to −1…+1

The resting position is never exactly 32768, so we measure it at start-up (hands off!). Then we scale each axis to −1 … +1 and add a small dead zone so the colour doesn't wander when the stick is released.

3. Angle and distance

Think of the stick position as a point (x, y). Its distance from the centre is √(x² + y²) and its angle is atan2(y, x) — a function that gives the direction of a point in radians, which we convert to degrees 0–360.

4. Colour wheel

Angle → hue, distance → saturation (from white in the middle to full colour at the edge). We reuse hsv_to_rgb() from Day 4. The button switches between "colour wheel" and "freeze" mode, so you can pick a colour and keep it.

🧠What you learned

  • Joysticks as two voltage dividers; internal pull-up resistors.
  • Calibrating a centre point, scaling, dead zones.
  • Cartesian → polar coordinates with sqrt and atan2.

⭐Exercise: Simon Says

  1. Four directions, four colours: up = red, right = green, down = blue, left = yellow.
  2. The Pico shows a sequence of colours (start with 1). The player repeats it by pushing the stick in the matching directions and letting it spring back to the centre after each move.
  3. Correct → add one more random colour and play the longer sequence. Wrong → flash white three times, print the score (sequence length reached) and start over.
  4. A button press at any time restarts the game.
Hint

Write read_direction() that waits until the stick is pushed far (distance > 0.7), decides the direction by which axis is bigger and its sign, then waits until the stick is back in the centre before returning. Your joystick might be mounted rotated: print the directions first and swap signs if needed.

Show solution

A move only counts once the stick returns to the centre — otherwise holding it would count as many moves. If "up" comes out as "down" on your board, flip the sign in direction_of().

Raising a custom exception (Restart) is a neat way to jump out of several nested loops at once.
