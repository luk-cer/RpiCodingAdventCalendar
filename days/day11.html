11Temperature & Humidity

The little blue DHT11 measures temperature and how much water is in the air. You'll build a room-comfort meter on the traffic light and learn to handle sensors that sometimes fail to answer.

🧰You need

  • Pico W + breadboard
  • KY-015 DHT11 temperature & humidity sensor
  • LED Traffic Light module
  • 8 jumper wires

🔍Meet the DHT11

Inside the blue grille is a humidity-sensitive layer and a thermistor, plus a small chip that sends the results over one data wire using its own timing-based protocol. It is cheap and slow: one reading per second at most, ±2 °C and ±5 % relative humidity (RH). Relative humidity means: how full of water vapour is the air compared with the maximum it could hold at this temperature? Indoors, 40–60 % feels comfortable.

🔌Wiring

📓Code, step by step

1. First reading

MicroPython's built-in dht module does the tricky timing. measure() triggers a reading; then temperature() and humidity() return the values.

2. When sensors fail

Sometimes the DHT11 doesn't answer correctly (a bit got lost, or you asked too quickly). measure() then raises an OSError. Instead of crashing, we catch the error and try again.

3. Watch your breath

Run this and breathe gently onto the sensor. Humidity shoots up and slowly falls back as the sensor dries.

4. Comfort rules

We combine both values into one verdict. Writing it as a function that returns a word (instead of switching LEDs directly) keeps the logic testable — you can call comfort(21, 50) in the shell and check the answer.

⭐Exercise: dew point & breath detector

  1. Calculate the dew point — the temperature at which water would condense out of the air (the reason windows fog up). Use the Magnus formula:
    γ = ln(RH/100) + 17.62·T / (243.12 + T), then dew = 243.12·γ / (17.62 − γ).
  2. Print temperature, humidity and dew point. If the dew point is within 3 °C of the temperature, show red: "condensation risk!".
  3. Breath detector: if humidity jumps by 5 % or more between two readings, count it as a breath and blink the yellow LED. Print the number of breaths detected.
Hint

math.log is the natural logarithm. Remember the previous humidity in a variable last_h and compare. Read every 1 s for the breath detector to be responsive.

Show solution

At 21 °C and 50 % RH the dew point is about 10 °C — so a window colder than 10 °C fogs up. That's why bathrooms (high RH) steam up so easily.
