21Safe Cracker

A rotary encoder is a knob that turns forever and clicks as it goes. You'll decode its secret two-wire language, add satisfying tick sounds — and build a combination safe with the traffic light in the exercise.

🧰You need

  • Pico W + breadboard
  • KY-040 Rotary encoder
  • KY-006 Passive buzzer
  • LED Traffic Light module
  • 12 jumper wires

🔍How a rotary encoder talks

Inside are two switches, CLK (also called A) and DT (B), that open and close as you turn — but slightly out of step with each other. Turning clockwise, A changes first; anticlockwise, B changes first. Reading both tells you the direction. The pattern repeats 00 → 01 → 11 → 10 → 00 (one way) or backwards (the other way). This sequence is called Gray code: only one bit changes at a time. Pressing the knob clicks a third switch, SW.

🔌Wiring

📓Code, step by step

1. Watch the two signals

The KY-040 has pull-up resistors for CLK and DT on the board; the SW button often doesn't, so we add the Pico's internal pull-up.

Turn slowly one click clockwise, then one anticlockwise. You'll see the Gray code walk forwards, then backwards. Most KY-040s go through all four states per click and rest at (1, 1).

2. A transition table

Combine the old and new state into a 4-bit number old·4 + new (0…15). For each of the 16 possible transitions, the table says: +1 (one step clockwise), −1 (anticlockwise) or 0 (no change, or an impossible jump caused by bouncing — which we simply ignore). This makes the decoder very robust.

<< shifts bits to the left (x << 1 = x · 2) and | combines bits — a compact way to pack two pins into one number.

3. Steps per click

With four transitions per detent, the knob position is raw // 4. If one click moves your count by 2, set STEPS = 2; if it counts backwards, swap the CLK and DT pin numbers.

4. Tick sounds

A short, high click on the passive buzzer for each step, and a lower tone for the button — just like a safe's dial.

5. A dial from 0 to 19

Like a real safe, the dial wraps around: after 19 comes 0. The modulo operator % does that for us, even for negative numbers.

🧠What you learned

  • Quadrature / Gray code, and why encoders use two out-of-phase signals.
  • Table-driven decoding that ignores bounce automatically.
  • Bit operations << and |, integer division // and modulo %.

⭐Exercise: crack the safe

  1. The safe has a 3-number combination on the 0–19 dial, e.g. [7, 15, 3].
  2. Turn to a number and press the knob to enter it. Each correct entry lights yellow briefly and plays a rising tone. A wrong entry plays a low buzz, flashes red and resets to the first number.
  3. After all three are correct: green light and a little victory melody. Pressing again re-locks the safe (red).
  4. Bonus "safecracker hint": when the dial is exactly on the next correct number, the tick sounds slightly different. Real safecrackers listen for that!
Hint
